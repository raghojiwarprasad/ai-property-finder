# Golden lesson 01: vertical and horizontal AI

Status: authored teaching example for review; not a complete published curriculum. Use case: UC01 or UC02. Capability: distinguish broad cross-domain capabilities from domain-specific workflows. Source profile fixture: “Coordinated suppliers and compared bids for construction projects.” The scenario below is fictional.

## Learner-facing lesson

Imagine your construction team uses one AI assistant to draft emails, summarise meeting notes and rewrite reports. Those capabilities are useful across construction, retail, education and many other industries. This is a horizontal use of AI: a broad capability serving many contexts.

Now imagine a product designed around construction tender review. It understands the required document structure, compares bids against project requirements, flags missing compliance documents and fits into the team's approval process. That is a vertical AI application: a product built around a particular domain and workflow.

The same underlying model could power both. The distinction is mainly the application, workflow, data and domain requirements; it does not mean that one uses “real AI” and the other does not. The boundary can be fuzzy. A general product can have industry-specific features, and a domain product can use general capabilities.

You already know that buying a general spreadsheet tool and buying a construction estimating system are different decisions. The AI version has a similar distinction, while also requiring attention to model errors, evidence and controls.

## Worked example

A tool that turns rough notes into polished text for any profession is primarily horizontal. A tool that checks construction tender submissions against the required technical documents and routes exceptions to a project reviewer is a vertical application. Neither description proves that the tool is accurate or worth buying; those need separate evaluation.

## Independent task

Classify the following and explain what information would change your answer:

1. An assistant that summarises uploaded documents for people in many industries.
2. A system that extracts clauses from construction contracts, compares them against project requirements and routes exceptions for review.
3. The first assistant adds a construction prompt template but no specialised workflow or validation.

Expected reasoning: 1 is primarily horizontal; 2 is vertical; 3 remains mainly horizontal with a domain-oriented entry point, though the boundary depends on the depth of domain functionality. Accept a nuanced answer that states its criteria. Do not require identical wording.

## Rubric

- Critical: distinguishes broad capability from domain/workflow specialisation.
- Critical: does not claim a different foundational model is required for vertical AI.
- Supporting: recognises that a prompt template alone may be shallow specialisation.
- Supporting: does not infer accuracy or business value from the category alone.

Pass: both critical criteria and at least one supporting criterion. A model-only explanation fails the relevant critical criterion. Borderline reasoning is flagged for review instead of forced into a binary category.

## Adaptation

If correct, move to choosing an appropriate workflow and evaluation criteria. If the learner says vertical means a more powerful model, show the same model used in two differently designed products. If the learner categorises by company size, contrast two products from the same vendor. If they ask for technical depth, discuss domain data, retrieval, schema validation and integrations without claiming every vertical product contains all of them.

Transfer task: classify a general writing assistant and a system designed for insurance-claim document review. This is a new domain; it must not repeat the construction wording. A later independent explanation can contribute to the evidence contract.
